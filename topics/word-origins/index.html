10 word origins questions

1 · Warm-up

The word robot came through Czech from robota, which is closest in meaning to what?

  1. A clockwork toy
  2. A clever servant
  3. Forced labor
  4. A metal machine
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Forced labor. Robot derives from a word for forced labor.

Karel Čapek's 1920 play popularized robot for artificial workers. The term came from Czech robota, meaning forced labor or drudgery.

Sources: Merriam-Webster and Science Museum.

2 · Warm-up

The Italian expression behind quarantine referred to how many days?

  1. Seven
  2. Fourteen
  3. Thirty
  4. Forty
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Forty. Quarantine derives from the Italian word for forty.

Venetian authorities required arriving ships to wait forty days during plague outbreaks. The English word preserves the Italian quaranta, meaning “forty.”

Sources: Merriam-Webster and Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

3 · Core

Captain Charles Boycott held which occupation when he was socially and economically isolated in Ireland?

  1. A land agent
  2. A factory owner
  3. A newspaper editor
  4. A shipbuilder
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: A land agent. Charles Boycott was a land agent.

In 1880, tenants and local workers isolated land agent Charles Boycott during a rent dispute. Newspapers then turned his surname into a term for organized refusal.

Sources: Merriam-Webster and Oxford Learner's Dictionaries.

4 · Core

During the American Civil War, what feature of a prison camp was called a deadline?

  1. A meal cutoff
  2. A boundary line
  3. A railroad timetable
  4. A work quota
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: A boundary line. A deadline marked a boundary prisoners could not cross.

At Civil War prison camps, a deadline marked a boundary that prisoners were forbidden to pass; guards could fire on violators. The word later came to mean a time limit.

Sources: Merriam-Webster and National Park Service.

5 · Core

The Latin word musculus is a diminutive name for which animal?

  1. A little fox
  2. A tiny snake
  3. A little mouse
  4. A small bird
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: A little mouse. Musculus means “little mouse.”

Latin musculus means “little mouse.” The name likely referred to the appearance of a contracting muscle moving beneath the skin.

Sources: Académie française and Oxford Learner's Dictionaries.

6 · Core

The word daisy preserves which Old English image?

  1. Sun's crown
  2. Meadow's clock
  3. Morning star
  4. Day's eye
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Day's eye. Daisy derives from “day's eye.”

Old English dæges ēage literally meant “day's eye.” The name refers to the flower's yellow center and its habit of opening in daylight.

Sources: Merriam-Webster and Oxford Learner's Dictionaries.

7 · Core

What kind of nighttime visitor did the older word mare describe?

  1. An evil spirit
  2. A dark horse
  3. A storm cloud
  4. A sleepless hour
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: An evil spirit. The older mare was an attacking spirit.

The older mare was an evil spirit believed to sit on sleepers and cause suffocation or frightening dreams. The word is unrelated to the term for a female horse despite the identical spelling.

Sources: Merriam-Webster and Online Etymology Dictionary.

8 · Core

Which outfit identified a Roman candidatus seeking public office?

  1. A red military cloak
  2. A chalk-whitened toga
  3. Gold-trimmed sandals
  4. A black mourning robe
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: A chalk-whitened toga. Roman candidates wore bright white togas.

Latin candidatus meant a person dressed in white, from candidus, meaning “bright white.” Roman office seekers whitened their togas to stand out in public.

Sources: Merriam-Webster and University of Chicago Logeion.

9 · Challenge

The phrase “and per se and” eventually fused into which word?

  1. Alphabet
  2. Asterisk
  3. Ampersand
  4. Et cetera
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Ampersand. Ampersand is a contraction of “and per se and.”

The & symbol was recited as “and per se and,” meaning “and by itself, and.” Repeated pronunciation gradually contracted the phrase into ampersand.

Sources: Merriam-Webster and Oxford Learner's Dictionaries.

10 · Challenge

Which government office did Étienne de Silhouette hold?

  1. Royal portraitist
  2. Paris printer
  3. Theater costumer
  4. French finance minister
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: French finance minister. Étienne de Silhouette was a finance minister.

Étienne de Silhouette was Louis XV's finance minister and was associated with strict economy. Inexpensive profile portraits were called à la Silhouette, preserving his surname in the term.

Sources: Smithsonian American Art Museum and Merriam-Webster.
