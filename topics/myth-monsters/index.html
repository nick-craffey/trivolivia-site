10 mythological creatures questions

1 · Warm-up

Medusa had two Gorgon sisters, Stheno and Euryale. What made her uniquely vulnerable to Perseus?

  1. She was mortal
  2. She lacked a petrifying gaze
  3. She had no wings
  4. She lived apart from them
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: She was mortal. Medusa was the only mortal Gorgon sister.

Stheno and Euryale were immortal, but Medusa was mortal. That distinction made her the Gorgon whom Perseus could kill.

Sources: The Metropolitan Museum of Art and National Archaeological Museum, Athens.

2 · Warm-up

In his best-known three-headed form, Cerberus guarded Hades mainly to prevent whom from crossing?

  1. The gods from entering
  2. The dead from escaping
  3. Heroes from dying
  4. Giants from waking
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: The dead from escaping. Cerberus prevented the dead from leaving Hades.

Cerberus guarded the entrance to Hades and caught or devoured shades that tried to escape. Some ancient accounts gave him more than three heads, although three became the standard depiction.

Sources: Acropolis Museum and MIHO Museum.

3 · Core

After Theseus killed the Minotaur, Ariadne’s gift solved which remaining problem?

  1. Proving his royal blood
  2. Calming King Minos
  3. Retracing the labyrinth
  4. Sailing past the Sirens
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Retracing the labyrinth. Ariadne's thread allowed Theseus to retrace his route.

Theseus unrolled Ariadne's thread as he entered the labyrinth. After killing the Minotaur, he followed the line back to the exit.

Sources: The Metropolitan Museum of Art and The British Museum.

4 · Core

Thor disguised himself as Freyja and entered the giant Thrym’s wedding feast to recover what?

  1. Odin’s missing eye
  2. Freyr’s magic ship
  3. Freyja’s necklace
  4. His stolen hammer
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: His stolen hammer. Thor used the disguise to recover his stolen hammer.

Thrym hid Mjölnir and demanded Freyja as his bride. Thor took her place in disguise; when the hammer was produced to bless the wedding, he seized it back.

Sources: Swedish History Museum and World History Encyclopedia.

5 · Core

In ancient Greek art, Sirens were typically women combined with which animal—not fish?

  1. Birds
  2. Horses
  3. Lions
  4. Snakes
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Birds. Ancient Greek artists depicted Sirens with bird bodies.

Homer never described the Sirens’ bodies, but ancient Greek artists gave them human heads and bird bodies. Their mermaid-like image developed much later.

Sources: The Metropolitan Museum of Art and National Audubon Society.

6 · Core

In traditional Chinese imagery, a dragon was often welcomed because it could bring what?

  1. Safe sea voyages
  2. Life-giving rain
  3. A rich ore strike
  4. Protection from winter
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Life-giving rain. Chinese dragons were associated with rain and successful harvests.

Chinese dragons were often powerful but benevolent beings associated with rain and bountiful harvests. Their command of water also helped link them to imperial rule.

Sources: RISD Museum and Norton Museum of Art.

7 · Core

In Irish folklore, hearing a banshee’s wail most often meant she was doing what?

  1. Driving intruders away
  2. Calling fairies to battle
  3. Warning that a death was near
  4. Causing the listener’s death
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Warning that a death was near. The banshee's wail warned that a death was near.

Traditional accounts treat the banshee as a death omen whose wail heralds a loss, often for a particular family. She predicts the death rather than causing it.

Sources: National Folklore Collection, University College Dublin and Smithsonian Center for Folklife and Cultural Heritage.

8 · Core

According to the Egyptian judgment scene, what happened to a heart that failed to balance against Ma'at's feather?

  1. Crown its owner
  2. Return it to the body
  3. Lock it in a tomb
  4. Devour the heart
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Devour the heart. Ammit would devour a heart that failed the test.

Ammit combined crocodile, lion, and hippopotamus traits. An unworthy heart was fed to her, condemning its owner to nonexistence rather than an afterlife.

Sources: The British Museum and Brooklyn Museum.

9 · Challenge

According to medieval bestiaries, which ordinary animal could hunt and kill a basilisk?

  1. A weasel
  2. A raven
  3. A toad
  4. A hedgehog
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: A weasel. Medieval bestiaries identified the weasel as the basilisk's adversary.

Bestiaries claimed a weasel could pursue the lethal basilisk into its hole and kill it. Writers turned the matchup into a lesson that every evil has a remedy.

Sources: The British Library and The Morgan Library & Museum.

10 · Challenge

The eaglelike Garuda, mount of Vishnu, is traditionally shown fighting which beings?

  1. Rakshasas, demon beings
  2. Nagas, serpent beings
  3. Gandharvas, sky musicians
  4. Yakshas, nature spirits
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Nagas, serpent beings. Garuda's traditional adversaries are the serpent beings called nagas.

Hindu tradition makes Garuda Vishnu’s divine vehicle and a frequent foe of nagas. One account calls them stepbrothers whose rivalry began with their mothers.

Sources: Herbert F. Johnson Museum of Art, Cornell University and The British Museum.
