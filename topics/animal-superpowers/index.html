10 animal biology questions

1 · Warm-up

How many hearts does an octopus have?

  1. One
  2. Two
  3. Three
  4. Four
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Three. An octopus has three hearts.

Two hearts pump blood through the gills, while the third sends oxygenated blood around the body. Each heart has a different job.

Sources: Smithsonian Ocean and NOAA Ocean Service.

2 · Warm-up

An owl must turn its head to change its gaze because its eyes have which structural feature?

  1. Round and side-facing
  2. Flat and forward-facing
  3. Loose in wide sockets
  4. Tubular and fixed
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Tubular and fixed. Owls have elongated, tube-shaped eyes fixed in their sockets.

An owl's tube-shaped eyes stay fixed rather than rotating freely. It turns its head to look in another direction.

Sources: Smithsonian Science Education Center and National Audubon Society.

3 · Core

Which bird can sustain controlled backward flight?

  1. A hummingbird
  2. A kingfisher
  3. A swift
  4. A kestrel
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: A hummingbird. Hummingbirds can sustain controlled backward flight.

Unlike most birds, hummingbirds generate lift on both the downstroke and upstroke. Their rotating wings let them hover and fly backward.

Sources: Smithsonian's National Zoo and Cornell Lab of Ornithology.

4 · Core

How can a dolphin keep surfacing to breathe while it sleeps?

  1. Both brain halves sleep for seconds only
  2. One brain hemisphere sleeps at a time
  3. Breathing continues as a fully automatic reflex
  4. Sleep happens only while resting at the surface
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: One brain hemisphere sleeps at a time. Dolphins rest one brain hemisphere at a time.

One hemisphere rests while the other remains active enough to control breathing and monitor the surroundings. The two hemispheres alternate these roles.

Sources: NOAA Fisheries and National Park Service.

5 · Core

The reflective layer behind a reindeer's retina changes shade as winter darkness arrives. Which shift occurs?

  1. Blue to bright red
  2. Gold to silver
  3. Gold to deep blue
  4. Clear to violet
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Gold to deep blue. A reindeer's retinal reflector changes from gold to deep blue in winter.

The reflective layer behind the retina, called the tapetum lucidum, shifts from summer gold to winter blue. The change helps reindeer see in Arctic winter darkness.

Sources: University College London and National Geographic.

6 · Core

An archerfish knocks insects off branches above water. How does it make the shot?

  1. Flick water upward with its tail
  2. Blow a bubble through its gills
  3. Launch a pebble from its mouth
  4. Force a water jet through its mouth
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Force a water jet through its mouth. Archerfish knock insects down with precisely aimed jets of water.

The fish presses its tongue against a groove in the roof of its mouth, forming a tube. A precisely aimed stream knocks insects from branches into the water.

Sources: Aquarium of the Pacific and San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance.

7 · Core

A sea otter floats on its back with a hard-shelled clam. What does it use a rock for?

  1. Cracking open the clam
  2. Anchoring itself while sleeping
  3. Sharpening its teeth
  4. Marking a spot on the seafloor
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Cracking open the clam. Sea otters use stones to open hard-shelled prey.

Floating on its back, an otter can strike a clam against a rock on its chest. Loose skin under each forearm carries extra prey from a dive.

Sources: Monterey Bay Aquarium and Aquarium of the Pacific.

8 · Core

What can an axolotl do after losing a leg?

  1. Grow a flexible cartilage limb
  2. Rebuild a fully structured leg
  3. Form a skin-covered support stump
  4. Seal the wound with a bony cap
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Rebuild a fully structured leg. Axolotls can regenerate complete limbs.

Cells near the wound form a growth zone called a blastema. It directs the regrowth of a complete limb, rather than just sealing the wound.

Sources: National Institute of General Medical Sciences and Harvard Stem Cell and Regenerative Biology.

9 · Challenge

A snapping shrimp snaps its claw shut. What actually makes the loud sound?

  1. A pellet of shell fired forward
  2. An electrical arc through seawater
  3. A collapsing cavitation bubble
  4. A burst of concentrated venom
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: A collapsing cavitation bubble. The snap comes from the collapse of a cavitation bubble.

The claw shoots out a fast water jet, creating a cavitation bubble. Its violent collapse makes the snap and can produce a momentary flash of light.

Sources: Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution and Nature.

10 · Challenge

How does a bombardier beetle control the reaction that produces its hot defensive spray?

  1. It freezes each chemical before mixing
  2. It vents the reaction through its wings
  3. It stores the hot mixture in its stomach
  4. It mixes and fires the chemicals in rapid pulses
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: It mixes and fires the chemicals in rapid pulses. The beetle mixes and expels the chemicals in rapid pulses.

Separate chemicals meet in a reinforced chamber, where pressure briefly closes the inlet and forces a hot burst outward. The cycle repeats in rapid pulses.

Sources: MIT News and Natural History Museum.
