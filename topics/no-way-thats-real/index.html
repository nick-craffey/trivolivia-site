10 scientific phenomena questions

1 · Warm-up

By total area, which place is Earth's largest desert?

  1. The Sahara
  2. The Gobi
  3. Antarctica
  4. The Arabian Desert
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Antarctica. Antarctica is Earth's largest desert.

A desert is defined by scarce precipitation, not heat or sand. Antarctica receives very little snowfall and spans about 14.2 million square kilometers.

Sources: British Antarctic Survey and Natural History Museum, London.

2 · Warm-up

Botanically, which fruit is a true berry even though a strawberry is not?

  1. A peach
  2. An apple
  3. A mango
  4. A banana
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: A banana. A banana is botanically a berry, while a strawberry is not.

A botanical berry develops from one flower with one ovary and has fleshy layers around its seeds. Bananas qualify; strawberries are aggregate accessory fruits.

Sources: McGill University Office for Science and Society and University of California Agriculture and Natural Resources.

3 · Core

A wombat is the only known animal to routinely leave droppings in what shape?

  1. Cubes
  2. Spirals
  3. Hollow rings
  4. Flat discs
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Cubes. Wombats routinely produce cube-shaped droppings.

Dry material and unevenly stiff regions of the intestine form the corners before exit. The flat sides may help territorial markers stay put on rocks and logs.

Sources: Georgia Institute of Technology and Cleland Wildlife Park, Government of South Australia.

4 · Core

From a vantage point high above water droplets, what complete shape can a rainbow form?

  1. A wider semicircle
  2. A complete circle
  3. A flattened oval
  4. Two concentric arcs
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: A complete circle. A rainbow can appear as a complete circle.

Sunlight refracts and reflects inside water droplets. From the ground, the horizon blocks the lower part; high above droplets, a viewer may see the rainbow close into a complete circle.

Sources: NOAA NESDIS and UK Met Office.

5 · Core

At Oklo, ore was depleted in uranium-235 and contained fission products. What process had occurred?

  1. A meteorite had mixed into the ore
  2. The deposit had partly melted
  3. A natural fission chain reaction had run
  4. Groundwater had carried uranium away
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: A natural fission chain reaction had run. A natural fission chain reaction occurred at Oklo.

About two billion years ago, uranium-rich rock at Oklo reached criticality. Groundwater slowed neutrons enough to maintain fission, leaving depleted uranium-235 and fission products behind.

Sources: International Atomic Energy Agency and U.S. Geological Survey.

6 · Core

Shark ancestors date to about 450 million years ago and the first trees to about 385 million. What is the approximate difference?

  1. 15 million years
  2. 35 million years
  3. 100 million years
  4. 65 million years
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: 65 million years. Shark ancestors predated the first trees by about 65 million years.

Shark-like scales appear around 450 million years ago, while the first trees arrived around 385 million years ago. Subtracting the dates gives a 65-million-year gap.

Sources: Natural History Museum, London and New York State Museum.

7 · Core

What movement occurs after the sun heats one side of the Eiffel Tower?

  1. It expands and leans away
  2. It contracts and leans toward
  3. It rotates slightly eastward
  4. It stays exactly the same
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: It expands and leans away. Solar heating makes the Eiffel Tower expand and lean away from the heat.

Heat expands the iron, making the tower a few millimeters taller. Uneven heating also makes its top trace a curve about 15 centimeters across during a clear day.

Sources: Société d’Exploitation de la Tour Eiffel and OpenStax.

8 · Core

Which word names the earthy scent after rain?

  1. Geosmin
  2. Petrichor
  3. Ozone
  4. Humus
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Petrichor. The word is petrichor.

CSIRO chemists coined petrichor in 1964 from Greek petra, stone, and ichor, the gods’ ethereal blood. Moisture releases the characteristic scent from soil and rock.

Sources: Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation and American Chemical Society.

9 · Challenge

While a wood frog is frozen, what happens to its heartbeat?

  1. It speeds up and stays fast
  2. It slows but never stops
  3. It stops, then restarts on thawing
  4. It continues in short bursts
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: It stops, then restarts on thawing. A wood frog's heart stops during freezing and restarts during thawing.

Glucose helps protect the frog's cells while much of its body freezes. Breathing and heartbeat stop; during thawing, the heart is among the first organs to resume function.

Sources: U.S. National Park Service and Yale Peabody Museum.

10 · Challenge

At water’s triple point, which three phases coexist in equilibrium?

  1. Liquid, vapor, and plasma
  2. Ice, liquid, and plasma
  3. Ice, vapor, and plasma
  4. Ice, liquid, and vapor
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Ice, liquid, and vapor. Ice, liquid water, and water vapor coexist at the triple point.

At about 0.01°C and 4.6 torr, solid, liquid, and gas coexist in equilibrium. Triple-point cells turn that precise condition into a temperature reference.

Sources: National Institute of Standards and Technology and Purdue University Department of Chemistry.
