10 historical context questions

1 · Warm-up

Teaching at Oxford began around 1096, more than two centuries before the founding of which major city?

  1. Tenochtitlan
  2. Cairo
  3. Kyoto
  4. Venice
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Tenochtitlan. Oxford teaching predates Tenochtitlan by more than two centuries.

Teaching at Oxford is documented from 1096. The Mexica founded Tenochtitlan in 1325—more than two centuries later—while the other listed cities were already established.

Sources: University of Oxford and Encyclopaedia Britannica.

2 · Warm-up

Approximately how long did the Anglo-Zanzibar War last?

  1. About 5 minutes
  2. About 40 minutes
  3. About 6 hours
  4. About 2 days
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: About 40 minutes. The fighting lasted roughly forty minutes.

Accounts commonly place the fighting at about 38 to 45 minutes. A British bombardment quickly disabled the palace defenses, making it the shortest war on record.

Sources: Historical Association and Guinness World Records.

3 · Core

In 1932, Australian soldiers were sent to control which animals?

  1. Feral camels
  2. Wild dogs
  3. Emus
  4. Rabbits
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Emus. The operation targeted large flocks of emus.

Soldiers used Lewis guns against emus eating crops in Western Australia. The birds dispersed into small groups and were difficult to target, so the military campaign was withdrawn.

Sources: Government of Western Australia, Department of Biodiversity, Conservation and Attractions and State Library of Western Australia.

4 · Core

The failed Boston storage tank released which liquid?

  1. Beer
  2. Lamp oil
  3. Milk
  4. Molasses
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Molasses. A wave of molasses moved through the surrounding streets.

About 2.3 million gallons escaped when the tank failed, producing a fast-moving wave that smashed buildings and killed 21 people. It became known as the Great Molasses Flood.

Sources: City of Boston and Smithsonian Magazine.

5 · Core

During the first months after Sweden's 1967 traffic change, how did road crashes change?

  1. Fell at first
  2. Stayed unchanged
  3. Doubled at once
  4. Rose for a decade
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Fell at first. Road crashes initially decreased.

Crashes and fatalities initially fell after Dagen H. Lower speed limits and unusually cautious driving contributed; the effect diminished as drivers became accustomed to the new system.

Sources: Stockholm City Museum and M Sverige.

6 · Core

The complaint from Ur described which poor-quality material?

  1. Beer
  2. Copper
  3. Wool
  4. Pottery
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Copper. The complaint concerned inferior copper.

Nanni's tablet criticizes the merchant Ea-nasir for supplying inferior copper and treating his messenger poorly. It also asserts the customer's right to reject future ingots.

Sources: British Museum and Guinness World Records.

7 · Core

Which later technology developed from Alexander Bain's 1843 image-transmission patent?

  1. The phonograph
  2. Radio
  3. The fax machine
  4. Television
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: The fax machine. The patent was an ancestor of fax transmission.

Alexander Bain patented an electric printing telegraph in 1843, establishing an early form of fax transmission. Bell's telephone patent followed in 1876.

Sources: Engineering and Technology History Wiki and Smithsonian National Museum of American History.

8 · Core

How did Didius Julianus persuade the Praetorian Guard to support him after Pertinax was killed?

  1. He promised to avenge Pertinax
  2. He claimed Pertinax named him
  3. He offered the Guard land
  4. He offered the highest payment
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: He offered the highest payment. Julianus secured support through a competitive bid.

In 193 CE, the Praetorian Guard invited bids for its support. Julianus promised the soldiers more money than his rival and then ruled for only about nine weeks.

Sources: Cambridge University Press and World History Encyclopedia.

9 · Challenge

Which valuable food did Pepys bury with his papers?

  1. Parmesan cheese
  2. A barrel of ale
  3. A sack of flour
  4. A smoked ham
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Parmesan cheese. Pepys buried his Parmesan cheese.

Pepys recorded digging a pit for his wine and Parmesan cheese as the fire approached in 1666. Imported Parmesan was sufficiently valuable to protect in this way.

Sources: London Museum and Royal Museums Greenwich.

10 · Challenge

Which material was used for the first-place medals at the 1912 Summer Games?

  1. Gold-plated silver
  2. Solid gold
  3. Gilded bronze
  4. Gold-copper alloy
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Solid gold. Stockholm awarded the last solid-gold Olympic medals.

Stockholm's champions received medals made of solid gold. Modern Olympic gold medals are primarily silver with a thin gold coating.

Sources: Guinness World Records and World Gold Council.
