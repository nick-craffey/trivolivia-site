10 film production questions

1 · Warm-up

What production method made Toy Story a feature-film first?

  1. Animated with clay models
  2. Painted directly onto film
  3. Created with computer animation
  4. Filmed with miniature puppets
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Created with computer animation. Toy Story was the first fully computer-animated feature.

Pixar rendered every frame digitally, producing the first feature-length film made entirely with computer animation. The 1995 film took about four years to make.

Sources: Pixar Animation Studios and Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences.

2 · Warm-up

Psycho needed blood to look dark as it swirled down a black-and-white drain. What did Hitchcock use?

  1. Stage blood
  2. Red ink
  3. Tomato soup
  4. Chocolate syrup
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Chocolate syrup. The shower scene in Psycho used diluted chocolate syrup as blood.

Color accuracy did not matter in black and white; thickness and contrast did. Chocolate syrup gave Hitchcock the dark, viscous flow the camera needed.

Sources: British Film Institute and American Film Institute.

3 · Core

Which recorded sound supplied the steady hum at the heart of a Star Wars lightsaber?

  1. An old movie-projector motor
  2. A fluorescent lamp warming up
  3. A vacuum cleaner heard through a wall
  4. A jet turbine played backward
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: An old movie-projector motor. The steady lightsaber hum began with a film projector motor.

Ben Burtt blended an aging projector's motor with electrical buzz captured near a television. Moving a microphone past a speaker added the swinging whoosh.

Sources: StarWars.com and The Guardian.

4 · Core

A key high-pitched layer in Jurassic Park's T. rex roar came from which real animal?

  1. A full-grown lion
  2. A baby elephant
  3. An elephant seal
  4. A bald eagle
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: A baby elephant. A baby elephant supplied a high-pitched layer of the T. rex roar.

Sound designer Gary Rydstrom layered a rare baby-elephant trumpet over lower tiger sounds. Other T. rex textures included alligator gurgles.

Sources: National Public Radio and Smithsonian Magazine.

5 · Core

Gandalf and Frodo share a shot, yet one looks twice as tall. How was that illusion often made?

  1. Gandalf wore weighted platform boots
  2. The lens stretched only half the frame
  3. Actors stood at forced-perspective distances
  4. Frodo performed inside a glass box
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Actors stood at forced-perspective distances. Forced perspective created the apparent height difference in camera.

Placing actors at carefully different distances made equal-height performers look radically different. Moving platforms could preserve the illusion as the camera moved.

Sources: American Society of Cinematographers and Live Design.

6 · Core

How was the rotating hallway fight in Inception filmed?

  1. Actors performed underwater
  2. The camera image was flipped in editing
  3. Wires pulled actors around a fixed set
  4. The entire hallway set rotated 360 degrees
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: The entire hallway set rotated 360 degrees. The production rotated a 100-foot hallway set around the actors.

A 100-foot corridor rotated around the actors while a camera moved with or beside it. The performers' real falls and changes in footing created the apparent shifts in gravity.

Sources: British Academy of Film and Television Arts and American Society of Cinematographers.

7 · Core

Why was the mechanical shark in Jaws called “Bruce”?

  1. It was named after Spielberg's lawyer
  2. It honored a shark researcher
  3. It reused the stunt diver's nickname
  4. It matched the prop builder's middle name
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: It was named after Spielberg's lawyer. The shark was named after Spielberg's lawyer, Bruce Ramer.

The 25-foot mechanical shark took the name of attorney Bruce Ramer. Repeated saltwater failures led Spielberg to suggest the shark's presence without showing it directly.

Sources: Academy Museum of Motion Pictures and PBS.

8 · Core

In The Wizard of Oz, which suspended material created the tornado effect across the miniature farm?

  1. A spinning glass tube filled with smoke
  2. A 35-foot muslin funnel like a windsock
  3. A painted rope dragged through sand
  4. A column of cotton wrapped around a fan
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: A 35-foot muslin funnel like a windsock. The tornado was created with a large muslin windsock.

The funnel hung from a moving overhead gantry while its base followed a slot below. Dust leaking through the fabric softened its edges into a storm.

Sources: PBS American Experience and American Society of Cinematographers.

9 · Challenge

Mary Poppins used a glowing yellow backing. Why was sodium-vapor light especially useful?

  1. Yellow made every costume look warmer
  2. Color film could not record blue indoors
  3. Its narrow wavelength made a precise separate matte
  4. Animators planned to paint every sky gold
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: Its narrow wavelength made a precise separate matte. A narrow yellow wavelength enabled precise image mattes.

A special prism split sodium-yellow light onto separate film while recording actors in normal color. Fine edges, motion blur, and transparent objects survived the composite.

Sources: The Walt Disney Company and United States Patent and Trademark Office.

10 · Challenge

Neo bends around bullets as the viewpoint sweeps past him. How did the original “bullet time” rig do it?

  1. One ultra-fast camera circled on a rail
  2. A rotating mirror filmed a motionless actor
  3. A computer invented every view from one photo
  4. An array of still cameras fired in sequence
Show the answer, explanation, and sources

Answer: An array of still cameras fired in sequence. The original bullet-time rig used approximately 120 still cameras in sequence.

Cameras arranged along a curve captured adjacent viewpoints in sequence. Interpolated frames joined the photographs into a continuous moving viewpoint around slowed action.

Sources: American Society of Cinematographers and Guinness World Records.
